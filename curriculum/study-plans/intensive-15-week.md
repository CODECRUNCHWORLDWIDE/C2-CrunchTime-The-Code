# C2 — 15-Week Intensive Study Plan

**~36 hours per week · 540 hours total · The fast lane.**

This is the **full-time** pathway. It assumes you have ~36 hours a week available for ~4 months. It is appropriate if:

- You are between jobs and interviewing within 4–5 months.
- You are a bootcamp graduate prepping for the first interview cycle.
- You are a senior learner preparing for new-grad recruiting.
- You have time-bounded leave and a defined interview cycle ahead.

It is **not** appropriate if you are working full-time. For that, use [the 1-year mastery pathway](mastery-1-year.md). 36 hours a week on top of a job is a guaranteed burnout.

---

## Weekly rhythm

A representative week:

```
Monday        Tuesday       Wednesday     Thursday      Friday        Saturday      Sunday
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Lectures      Lectures      FRAME        Pattern       Mock prep     Mini-project  Quiz +
+ readings    + readings    drills        challenges    + portfolio   deep work     review
(6h)          (6h)          (8h)          (4h)          (3h)          (7h)          (2h)
```

Monday morning is the "set the table" session: read the week's three lectures, skim the resources, write your week's FRAME drill targets. Tuesday is your second reading + first hands-on. Wednesday and Thursday are the drill heart of the week — that's where pattern recognition becomes automatic. Friday is for mock-interview prep (peer scheduling, recording setup, behavioral story refinement). Saturday is mini-project deep work. Sunday is the quiz and a 1-hour reflection (write what clicked, what didn't, what to drill next week).

## If you fall behind

You will fall behind in at least one week. This is normal.

- **Behind by 1 day:** Compress Sunday's quiz/review. Drop one homework problem.
- **Behind by 2-3 days:** Drop the mini-project for that week. Catch up next weekend. Keep the mock if scheduled.
- **Behind by a week:** Stop. Don't try to "make it up by doing two weeks at once" — pattern recognition needs *time between exposures* to consolidate. Just shift the entire timeline by a week.

The streak metric is **deliberately not** "how many days in a row you did exactly the planned thing." It's "how many days in a row you did *something* substantive on C2." Pause your streak on a day off, fine. Keep it on a "5-minute review" day, fine.

## Pre-onsite acceleration (Weeks 14–15)

If you have actual interviews scheduled during Weeks 14 or 15, the priorities shift:

1. **Pattern recognition speed > new patterns.** Don't introduce new material in interview week. Drill recognition on patterns you already know.
2. **Mocks under real conditions.** Video on, no notes visible, full 45 minutes uninterrupted.
3. **Behavioral rehearsal daily.** Out loud. Recording yourself counts.
4. **Sleep > extra drills.** Tired-brain interviews are worse than rested-brain interviews even with less prep.
5. **One "review only" day** in the 24h before each interview. No new problems. Just re-read your strongest write-ups.

---

## Adapting

- **Have <15 weeks?** Cut Phase 1 to 2 weeks (compress Weeks 1-2 into one, Weeks 3-4 into one). You'll feel less grounded; you'll learn faster from mistakes. Tradeoff.
- **Have >15 weeks?** Insert review weeks at Weeks 5, 10. Re-drill earlier patterns. Do extra mocks. You will not regret it.

When the 15 weeks end, you go interview.
