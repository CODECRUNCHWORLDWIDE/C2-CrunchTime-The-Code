# C2 — 1-Year Mastery Pathway

**~10 hours per week · 52 weeks · ~520 hours total · The depth lane.**

This pathway covers exactly the same material as the [15-week intensive](intensive-15-week.md), spread across a calendar year. Same patterns, same problems, same FRAME Method, same capstone — but at a pace a working engineer can sustain.

---

## Why the mastery pace works

Two reasons.

**One:** pattern recognition is a *spaced-repetition* skill. When you see "two pointers" today and then again in 14 days and again in 30, your brain compresses the pattern in a way it can't if you binge ten two-pointer problems in a single weekend. The mastery pathway is engineered around that fact — every pattern returns at week +4 and week +12 for re-drilling.

**Two:** interview readiness is partly *psychological*. Twelve months of weekly FRAME practice produces a candidate who has *internalized* the framework. Fifteen weeks of full-time grinding produces a candidate who *learned* it. Under interview pressure, the difference matters.

Use this pathway if any of:

- You are working full-time and want to be interview-ready ~12 months from now.
- You are a college learner carrying a full course load.
- You finished the intensive once and want to keep skills sharp for a year.
- You are deliberately picking a year because depth > speed for you.

## Quarterly structure

The 52 weeks break into four 13-week quarters that mirror the intensive's four phases.

### Q1 (Weeks 1–13) — Foundations

| Mastery weeks | Intensive equivalent | Focus |
|--------------:|---------------------|-------|
| 1–3 | Week 1 | FRAME Method, two pointers, first 5 write-ups |
| 4–6 | Week 2 | Complexity, hash maps, re-drill two pointers |
| 7–9 | Week 3 | Sliding window, re-drill hash maps |
| 10–13 | Week 4 | Fast/slow pointers + **Mock #1** + Q1 review week |

### Q2 (Weeks 14–26) — Core patterns

| Mastery weeks | Intensive equivalent | Focus |
|--------------:|---------------------|-------|
| 14–16 | Week 5 | Binary search |
| 17–19 | Week 6 | BFS |
| 20–22 | Week 7 | DFS |
| 23–25 | Week 8 | Heaps / priority queues |
| 26 | — | Q2 review + **Mock #2** |

### Q3 (Weeks 27–39) — Advanced patterns

| Mastery weeks | Intensive equivalent | Focus |
|--------------:|---------------------|-------|
| 27–29 | Week 9 | Tries + advanced strings |
| 30–32 | Week 10 | Weighted graphs + union-find |
| 33–35 | Week 11 | Dynamic programming |
| 36–38 | Week 12 | Backtracking + combinatorics |
| 39 | — | Q3 review |

### Q4 (Weeks 40–52) — Capstone & onsite prep

| Mastery weeks | Intensive equivalent | Focus |
|--------------:|---------------------|-------|
| 40–42 | Week 13 | Behavioral, story bank |
| 43–45 | Week 14 | Bit manipulation, binary tries + **Mock #3** |
| 46–48 | Week 15 | Capstone build-up |
| 49–51 | — | Capstone, polish, mocks #4 |
| 52 | — | Recruiter pack, interview launch |

Each "3-week mastery block" maps to one "intensive week," giving you time to: read in week 1, drill in week 2, re-drill + write up in week 3.

## Weekly rhythm

A representative working-engineer week:

```
Mon (1h)    Tue (1.5h)  Wed (1.5h)  Thu (1.5h)  Fri (1h)   Sat (2.5h)  Sun (1h)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
read        FRAME      FRAME      Pattern     mock /     mini-       quiz +
lecture     drill       drill       challenge   peer       project /   review
                                                review     write-up
```

About 10 hours total. Spread across the week so no one day is a "marathon." This is *more sustainable* than a Saturday-only 10-hour block — pattern recognition needs repetition over days, not hours in one day.

## Streak system + dashboard

The mastery pathway is harder than the intensive *psychologically* because it lasts a year. The streak system is essential.

Your `progress.md` should include:

```markdown
# CrunchTime Mastery Progress

## Day-streak: 184 (longest: 184)
## Week-streak: 27 (longest: 27)
## Pattern coverage: 9 / 14
## FRAME write-ups: 32 / 60
## Mocks: 2 / 4
## Hours invested (running): 274 / 520

## This week's commitments (W28)
- [x] Mon — read sliding-window deep-dive (still re-drilling)
- [x] Tue — FRAME solve: minimum-window-substring
- [ ] Wed — FRAME solve: longest-repeating-character-replacement
- [ ] Thu — challenge problem: max-of-all-subarrays-of-size-k (hard)
- [ ] Fri — peer mock review on Mock #2
- [ ] Sat — write up Wednesday & Thursday problems
- [ ] Sun — week-28 reflection

## Rest days this month: 3 / 4 allowed
```

**Allowed rest:**

- **1 day per week** of zero C2 — preserves streak.
- **4 rest days per month** beyond the weekly day — also preserves streak.
- **2 weeks per year of full rest** — must be planned in advance and noted.
- **Sickness or emergencies** — pause without breaking. Resume when you can.

The point of the streak isn't "no days off." It's "no *invisible* days off." Plan them, take them, write them down.

## Peer / community rhythm

The mastery pathway works much better with peers. Suggested structure:

- **Find one peer doing C2 mastery** on a similar timeline. Discord, the Code Crunch community, your university, a coworker.
- **Weekly 30-minute call** every Friday or Sunday. Review each other's week. Schedule mocks.
- **Quarterly mock-interview swap.** You interview them. They interview you. Real feedback.
- **Capstone showcase** at end of year: present your portfolios to each other (or to a small community session).

If you can't find a peer, the [Code Crunch Discord community](https://github.com/CODECRUNCHWORLDWIDE) (link from the org) has a `#c2-mastery` channel.

## Common failure modes (and fixes)

**Skipping mocks because "I'm not ready yet."**
You are never ready for a mock. That's the point. Mocks at week 13 should feel terrible — that's the diagnostic data. Do them anyway.

**Re-doing the same easy problems instead of moving on.**
Comfort zone. If you can solve a problem in <10 minutes, it's no longer training. Move up a difficulty tier or a different pattern.

**Falling off in months 4-6.**
The "midpoint dip." Real and predictable. Antidote: schedule a celebration at month 3 (mock #1 done, Q1 badge), and another at month 6 (mock #2 done, Q2 badge). External markers help.

**Reading instead of solving.**
Reading other people's solutions feels productive and isn't. Cap solution-reading at 25% of total study time. If you spend 10 hours a week, that's 2.5 hours of reading, 7.5 hours of *your own typing*.

**Comparing your progress to someone else's.**
The mastery pathway is paced so that *you* can sustain it. If a peer in the intensive is ahead in week count, they're also burning out at 4x your rate. Don't envy.

## Switching pathways

You can move between pathways if your life changes.

- **Mastery → Intensive:** Possible if you have a hiring cycle suddenly approaching. From whichever quarter you're in, compress the remaining quarters into 4-week intensive blocks. You'll have skill gaps; do extra mocks to find them.
- **Intensive → Mastery:** Common (people start intensive, life happens, downshift). Convert the remaining weeks at 1:3.5 ratio. Take a 2-week break first to reset.

## What "done" looks like at month 12

When the 52 weeks end, you should have:

- 60+ FRAME write-ups committed.
- 4 recorded mocks.
- System-design write-up.
- Behavioral story bank with 12 STAR anecdotes.
- Recruiter-prep pack ready.
- A 4-week pre-onsite plan you've already used at least once.
- The **CrunchTime Graduate** badge in your portfolio repo.

You go interview.
