# Exercise 1 — Conflict / Disagreement (STAR)

> **Category:** Conflict / disagreement. **Target time:** 30 min to draft and revise; the spoken answer must come in under 2:00.

## STAR checklist (read before drafting)

- [ ] **Situation** is one to two sentences. If it runs longer, cut it.
- [ ] **Task** names *your specific* responsibility in one sentence, in "I."
- [ ] **Action** is ~60% of the answer, first-person, and — for a conflict story — *engages the other side's actual position* rather than just asserting you were right.
- [ ] **Result** lands a concrete outcome *and* a lesson about how you handle disagreement.
- [ ] The story is **true**. It will survive follow-ups ("what did they say back?").
- [ ] Spoken aloud, it comes in under **2:00**. You timed it.

---

## The prompt

> **"Tell me about a time you disagreed with a coworker, your manager, or a technical decision. How did you handle it?"**

This is the canonical Conflict prompt. Interviewers ask it in many phrasings — "a time you pushed back," "a technical decision you didn't agree with," "a disagreement on your team" — but they are all this category, and one prepared story answers all of them.

## What this question scores

The competency is **professional disagreement**: can you hold a different view, engage the other person's actual reasoning, and reach a resolution — without either steamrolling them or caving? The interviewer is *not* scoring whether you were right. They are scoring whether you disagree like someone they would want on their team.

## The trap to avoid

The "I was right and they were wrong" story. If your anecdote ends with "and it turned out I was correct all along," it scores poorly even if true — because it shows you treat disagreement as a contest to win rather than a problem to solve. The strong version shows you *genuinely engaged* the other position: you asked what was driving it, you took their constraint seriously, and the resolution was better than either starting point.

## Self-check before you look at SOLUTIONS

- Does your Action contain at least one sentence where you *engaged the other person's reasoning* (asked why, acknowledged a fair point, addressed their actual concern)? If not, you have written an "I was right" story — revise.
