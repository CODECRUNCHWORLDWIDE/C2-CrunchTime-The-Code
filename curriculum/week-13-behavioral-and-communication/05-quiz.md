# Week 13 — Category-Recognition Quiz

Ten short prompts. **Do not answer them.** For each, decide which of the eight behavioral categories applies — Conflict, Failure, Leadership, Teamwork, Ambiguity, Pressure, Influence, Growth (or Opener) — and name, in one line, what competency the interviewer is scoring and the one trap to avoid. This is the Match step for behavioral; the whole skill is the five-second classification.

Answer key at the bottom. Time yourself — 30 seconds per question is the target.

---

**Q1.** "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager about how to approach a problem."

**Q2.** "Tell me about a project where the requirements were vague and you had to figure out what to build."

**Q3.** "Tell me about a significant mistake you made and what you did about it."

**Q4.** "Tell me about a time you convinced a team to adopt an approach they were initially against, when you weren't the one in charge."

**Q5.** "Tell me about yourself."

**Q6.** "Tell me about a time you took the lead on something that nobody had been assigned to do."

**Q7.** "Tell me about a time you had to hit a tight deadline and couldn't finish everything you wanted to."

**Q8.** "Tell me about a piece of critical feedback you received and how you responded to it."

**Q9.** "Tell me about a time you worked with a teammate who wasn't pulling their weight."

**Q10.** "Tell me about something you're proud of building or accomplishing."

## Answer key

<details>
<summary>Click after attempting all ten</summary>

1. **Conflict.** Scores: professional disagreement — can you hold a different view, engage the other side, and resolve it. Trap: the "I was right, they were wrong" story; the strong answer engages the manager's actual reasoning. Lecture 1 §2 (category 1).

2. **Ambiguity.** Scores: making progress without a complete spec. Trap: a story where the ambiguity resolved itself or someone else resolved it; the strong answer shows *you* structured the ambiguity (sharp questions, validated assumptions, shipped to learn). Lecture 1 §2 (category 5).

3. **Failure.** Scores: ownership and learning — not whether you failed (everyone has). Trap: the fake failure ("I'm a perfectionist") or blame-shifting; the strong answer owns a real mistake and names the specific change made afterward. Lecture 1 §2 (category 2). Exercise 2 exactly.

4. **Influence.** Scores: moving a decision through persuasion and data, not authority. The prompt explicitly removes your authority ("you weren't in charge"), which is the signal. Trap: "I told them to and they did" (that's authority); the strong answer shows you understood their concern and brought evidence. Lecture 1 §2 (category 7).

5. **Opener.** Not one of the eight scored competencies — it is the frame for the whole round. Structure: present → past → future, ~90 seconds, tailored to the role. Trap: a chronological résumé recital; the strong answer is a tight narrative arc pointing at *this* job. Lecture 3 §2.

6. **Leadership / initiative.** Scores: agency — moving things forward unprompted. The phrase "nobody had been assigned" is the signal. Trap: waiting for a formal-title story; the strong answer is any motion you created that wouldn't have happened otherwise. Lecture 1 §2 (category 3). Exercise 3 exactly.

7. **Pressure / deadlines.** Scores: judgment under constraint — what you cut, what you protected, how you communicated the trade-off. Trap: the 80-hour hero story (signals poor planning); the strong answer shows a deliberate, communicated trade-off. Lecture 1 §2 (category 6).

8. **Growth / feedback.** Scores: self-awareness and the capacity to change. Trap: choosing trivial feedback or getting defensive about it; the strong answer names real feedback and the concrete behavior change that followed. Lecture 1 §2 (category 8).

9. **Teamwork / collaboration.** Scores: making others effective and handling a difficult colleague professionally. Note: this *could* be framed as Conflict, but the dominant verb ("worked with a teammate") points at Teamwork — pick the dominant competency and let the conflict show through. Trap: throwing the teammate under the bus; the strong answer addresses the issue while protecting their standing. Lecture 1 §2 (categories 4, and the straddle note in §2).

10. **Leadership / initiative** (or a "win" framed to it). Scores: agency and impact. The open-ended "proud of" invites you to *choose* the framing — lead with a story where you drove something, and land a quantified result. Trap: a passive accomplishment narrated in "we"; the strong answer makes *you* the engine and quantifies the outcome. Lecture 1 §2 (category 3); the quantified-Result discipline from Lecture 2 §3.

## How to score

- **9–10 correct categories:** your Match step is reflexive. Move on to delivery and the bank.
- **7–8:** solid; review the two you missed and the trap for each. The straddle cases (Q9, Q10) are the usual misses.
- **≤ 6:** re-read Lecture 1 §2 and the eight-category cheatsheet in `resources.md`. Re-drill until you hit the verb-to-category mapping in under five seconds, the same way you drilled coding-pattern recognition in earlier weeks.

The category itself is only half the answer — the competency-and-trap is the other half, because knowing *what is being scored* is what lets you frame the story correctly. A candidate who classifies the category but misreads the competency tells a relevant-but-wrong-emphasis story. Drill both.
