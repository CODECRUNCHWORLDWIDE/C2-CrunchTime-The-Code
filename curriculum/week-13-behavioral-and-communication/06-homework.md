# Week 13 — Homework

Six practice prompts plus the rubric. Allow ~5 hours total. Draft each STAR answer (or, for Problem 6, the opener) from a *real* experience of your own, with the lectures closed; consult a lecture only after a 10-minute stuck-period on a single prompt.

The prompts are chosen to drill the categories the three exercises did **not** cover — Ambiguity, Pressure, Teamwork, Influence, Growth — plus the opener. Together with the three exercises (Conflict, Failure, Leadership), this homework gives you at least one drafted story per category, which is the floor for a complete story bank. By Sunday, the recognition-and-framing on each should be reflexive.

| # | Prompt | Category | Deliverable | Est. time |
|---|--------|----------|-------------|----------:|
| 1 | Underspecified problem | Ambiguity | STAR answer, < 2:00 spoken | 45 min |
| 2 | Tight deadline / cut scope | Pressure | STAR answer, < 2:00 spoken | 45 min |
| 3 | Difficult teammate | Teamwork | STAR answer, < 2:00 spoken | 45 min |
| 4 | Changed a decision without authority | Influence | STAR answer, < 2:00 spoken | 50 min |
| 5 | Hard feedback you acted on | Growth | STAR answer, < 2:00 spoken | 40 min |
| 6 | "Tell me about yourself" | Opener | Present-past-future, ~90s | 40 min |

Problems 1–5 are full STAR; Problem 6 is the opener. Each drafted answer is a candidate story-bank slot — keep them all.

## Problem 1 — Ambiguity

**Prompt.** "Tell me about a time you had to make progress on a problem when the requirements were unclear or the path forward wasn't defined."

**Category.** Ambiguity (Lecture 1 §2, category 5).

**What's scored.** Can you make progress without a complete spec? The strong answer shows you *structured* the ambiguity — asked the sharpest questions, made and validated assumptions, shipped something small to learn from — rather than freezing or waiting for someone else to resolve it.

**Hint.** Pick a story where *you* drove the clarification. The Action should contain the specific questions you asked and the specific assumptions you made and checked. The trap: a story where the ambiguity resolved itself.

**Acceptance.** Full STAR; Action ≈60%; Result a metric or concrete observable; under 2:00 spoken and timed.

---

## Problem 2 — Pressure / deadlines

**Prompt.** "Tell me about a time you were under a tight deadline and had to make a trade-off about what to deliver."

**Category.** Pressure (Lecture 1 §2, category 6).

**What's scored.** Judgment under constraint: what you cut, what you protected, how you communicated the trade-off. The strong answer shows a *deliberate* trade-off communicated early — not heroics.

**Hint.** The Action's center should be the trade-off decision itself: what you chose to drop, what you chose to protect, and why. Show you communicated it to stakeholders *before* the deadline, not after. The trap: the 80-hour hero story, which signals poor planning rather than good judgment.

**Acceptance.** Full STAR; the trade-off and its communication are explicit; Result lands; under 2:00, timed.

---

## Problem 3 — Teamwork / difficult teammate

**Prompt.** "Tell me about a time you worked with a teammate who was difficult to work with, or who wasn't contributing."

**Category.** Teamwork (Lecture 1 §2, category 4).

**What's scored.** Do you make the people around you more effective, and can you handle a difficult colleague professionally? The strong answer addresses the issue *and* protects the teammate's standing — no throwing them under the bus.

**Hint.** Show the conversation you had, framed around impact rather than accusation ("the deadline slips if X doesn't land" rather than "you're lazy"). Credit any contribution they did make. The trap: a story that is secretly about how great you are and how bad they were.

**Acceptance.** Full STAR; the teammate is handled professionally; your specific contribution is clear; under 2:00, timed.

---

## Problem 4 — Influence without authority

**Prompt.** "Tell me about a time you changed someone's mind or got buy-in for an idea when you had no authority to mandate it."

**Category.** Influence (Lecture 1 §2, category 7).

**What's scored.** Moving a decision through persuasion, data, and trust rather than position. This is the column most engineers find thinnest in their story bank — do the work to find a real one.

**Hint.** The Action must show you understanding the other party's *concern* first, then bringing evidence that addressed it. The trap: "I told them to and they did it," which is authority, not influence. If you genuinely cannot find an influence story, that is a signal — look harder across code reviews, design discussions, and re-prioritization conversations.

**Acceptance.** Full STAR; the persuasion mechanism (understanding + evidence) is explicit; under 2:00, timed.

---

## Problem 5 — Growth / feedback

**Prompt.** "Tell me about a piece of hard feedback you received and what you did differently afterward."

**Category.** Growth (Lecture 1 §2, category 8).

**What's scored.** Self-awareness and the capacity to change in response to feedback. The strong answer names *real* feedback (not a humble-brag) and a *concrete* behavior change that followed.

**Hint.** Pick feedback that actually stung a little — that's where the real growth stories live. The Result is the behavior change and evidence it stuck. The trap: choosing trivial feedback, or getting subtly defensive ("the feedback was a bit unfair, but...").

**Acceptance.** Full STAR; the feedback is real and the change is specific; under 2:00, timed.

---

## Problem 6 — The opener ("Tell me about yourself")

**Prompt.** "Tell me about yourself."

**Category.** Opener (Lecture 3 §2). Not a STAR story.

**What's scored.** Overall communication and fit; it frames the whole round. The strong answer is a tight present-past-future narrative tailored to *this* role.

**Hint.** Present (~25s): what you do now. Past (~35s): the two or three experiences relevant *to this role*, selected for relevance not chronology. Future (~30s): why this role is the next step, showing you researched it. The trap: a chronological résumé recital.

**Acceptance.** Present-past-future structure; ~90 seconds, timed; tailored to a specific role; not a résumé recital.

## Rubric

For each answer, your draft is graded on five dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight | What "yes" looks like |
|-----------|-------:|----------------------|
| Category fit | 20% | The story clearly answers the asked category; framed to the scored competency |
| STAR structure + airtime | 20% | Short Situation, owned Task, Action ≈60%, Result landed (opener: present-past-future) |
| "I" discipline | 15% | First-person Action; team credited without erasing your contribution |
| Result quality | 25% | A metric or concrete observable, plus the lesson — never an adjective ("it went well") |
| Timed delivery | 20% | Spoken aloud and timed; under 2:00 (opener: ~90s); flagged and trimmed if over |

The Result weight is the highest for a reason: across the homework, the most common shortfall is a strong Action that trails off into an unlanded Result. A landed, specific, quantified-or-observable Result is what converts a relevant story into a *scoring* answer.

## Suggested order

1. **Problem 5 (Growth)** first — feedback stories are usually the easiest to recall and warm you up.
2. **Problem 3 (Teamwork)** second — most people have a clear difficult-teammate memory.
3. **Problem 1 (Ambiguity)** third — drills the "structure the ambiguity" framing.
4. **Problem 2 (Pressure)** fourth — drills the explicit trade-off.
5. **Problem 4 (Influence)** fifth — the hardest for most; give it the most time.
6. **Problem 6 (Opener)** last — rehearse it after your stories are drafted, so the Past you select is informed by your strongest material.

If time runs out, prioritize Problems 4 and 6. Influence is the most commonly thin category, and the opener is asked in essentially every loop — both are high-yield.

## Acceptance

The week's homework is complete when:

- All six prompts have a drafted answer under `behavioral/c2-week-13/homework/`.
- Each STAR answer (Problems 1–5) has been timed aloud and comes in under 2:00; the opener (Problem 6) under ~90s.
- The quiz is taken and scored.
- The retrospective names the category whose story was hardest to build — that category gets a second story before any real loop.

The retrospective is the single most useful artifact this week. The category most candidates need more reps on after W13 is **Influence** — engineers have many "I did X" stories and few "I changed someone's mind" stories. If yours is a different category, name it; the gap drives your bank-building over the weekend. Combined with the three exercise categories, this homework leaves you with one drafted story per category — exactly the floor the mini-project's coverage matrix builds on.
