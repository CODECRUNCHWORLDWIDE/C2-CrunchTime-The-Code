# Challenge 1 — The Full 45-Minute Round

> Format: a complete behavioural round, delivered cold and recorded end to end · Time: 45 minutes on the clock, plus about 90 minutes of review · Difficulty: this is a mock, not a problem — the difficulty is the endurance · Why this one: five stories rehearsed one at a time is not the same as eight answers in a row with follow-ups, and the difference is exactly what Mock #3 tests next week.

<!-- deliverable-page: the answer is a recorded round and its review, not a program -->

## The Brief

The five exercises rehearsed one story at a time, with the recorder stopping
between takes. A real round does not stop. Eight questions, follow-ups you did
not choose, forty-five minutes of it, and by question six the concentration that
made question two sharp is gone.

That endurance is the thing being drilled here, and it cannot be drilled in
pieces. One continuous recording, no re-takes, and the review afterwards is where
the improvement happens.

The deliverable is the recording, the per-answer rubric scores, and a two-pass
review write-up.

## Starter

The questions and the rubric are the starter, and they are
below under Requirements. Read them once now — the order matters and you
should know the arc — and then do not read them again until the recorder is
running.

## Requirements

1. One continuous recording of roughly 45 minutes, six to eight questions
   answered cold.
2. Per-answer scores against the five-signal rubric below.
3. A two-pass review — content first, delivery second — written up.
4. Your two weakest answers re-recorded with the fixes applied.
5. Any coverage gap you find written down and carried into the mini-project.

### The questions, in this order

A real behavioral round is 6–8 questions across categories, usually opening with "tell me about yourself," moving through the meaty categories, and closing with "why us" and "your questions for me." This set mirrors that arc. Budget ~5–6 minutes per question including follow-ups.

1. **(Why-this-role / opener)** "Let's start with you — walk me through your background and what brings you here." *(Target: your 90-second "tell me about yourself" pitch. Practice landing it under 90 seconds.)*
2. **(Accomplishment)** "Tell me about the work you're proudest of." *(Deploy your strongest-number story — debugging or checkout-latency.)*
3. **(Conflict)** "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a coworker or your manager." *(Deploy your conflict story. Land the relationship outcome.)*
4. **(Failure)** "Tell me about a time you failed." *(Deploy your failure story. Own it; name the change. Be ready for the "what would you do with more time?" follow-up.)*
5. **(Leadership / influence)** "Tell me about a time you drove something forward without the authority to make people do it." *(Deploy your leadership story. The proof is that it stuck without you.)*
6. **(Ambiguity)** "Tell me about a time you had to make progress with unclear requirements." *(Deploy your ambiguity story. Make the explicit assumption visible.)*
7. **(Difficult person / Teamwork)** "Tell me about a difficult person you worked with, or a time teamwork was hard." *(Deploy a difficult-person or teamwork story. Empathy, not blame.)*
8. **(Why-us / close)** "Why this team, and what questions do you have for me?" *(Have two specific, genuine questions ready. 'Do you have questions?' is graded too — having none reads as disengaged.)*

If you run short on stories for a category, that is itself the finding — note the gap and fill it in the mini-project. You should *not* tell the same story for two different questions; if you find yourself reaching for the checkout-latency story for both #2 and #6, you have found a coverage hole.

### The rubric

Score each answer 0–2 on each of the five signals (0 = absent, 1 = present but weak, 2 = clearly surfaced). Max 10 per answer.

| Signal | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|--------|---|---|---|
| Ownership | All "we"; you watched it happen | Some "I", but role is fuzzy | Clear "I"; you drove it, decided, risked |
| Impact | No outcome stated | Vague outcome ("it went well") | A specific number tied to something that mattered |
| Self-awareness | None | A token "I'd do better" | A genuine trade-off or limit you'd revisit |
| Collaboration | Solo heroics or no people | People mentioned, role unclear | "We" for the outcome; credit shared; friction resolved |
| Growth | No lesson | Generic lesson ("be more careful") | A concrete habit/process you adopted, ideally proven |

Also track, per answer:

- **Length** — was it under ~100 seconds? Over two minutes is a rambling flag.
- **S/T over-spend** — did you start the Action within the first 20 seconds?
- **Structure** — did you signal the STAR shape, or free-associate?

A round averaging **8+/10 across answers** is interview-ready. **6–7** means a few categories need a sharper story or a tighter budget. **Below 6** means re-drill the weak categories before Mock #3.

## Constraints

- **One take, no stopping.** A real round does not let you start an answer again.
  The re-take happens in review, not in the room.
- **Forty-five minutes, whether or not it is going well.** Cutting it short when
  question four goes badly removes the only thing this exercise measures.
- **No notes in front of you.** If you cannot deliver a story from memory it is
  not in the bank yet, and finding that out here is the point.
- **No story twice.** If you reach for the same story for two questions you have
  found a coverage hole. Note it; do not paper over it.
- **Answer the question that was asked.** Redirecting to the story you prepared
  is visible from the outside and it costs more than a weaker on-topic answer.
- **The closing question counts.** "Do you have questions for me?" is graded.
  Having none reads as disengaged.

### Setup

You have two ways to run this, in order of preference:

1. **With a partner (best).** Pair with another C2 learner. They play the interviewer, reading the questions below in order, taking brief notes, and throwing one or two natural follow-ups per answer ("what specifically was your role?", "what would you do differently?"). Then swap and you interview them. Interviewing someone else is the fastest way to internalize the rubric — you will *hear* the missing "I" and the buried result in their answers, then catch them in yours.

2. **Solo (works fine).** Record yourself. Read each question aloud (or have a text-to-speech read it), pause, then answer from memory as if a real interviewer asked it. Hit record once and run the whole 45 minutes in one take — the point is to simulate the *endurance* of a real round, not to splice together perfect individual answers.

Either way: **one continuous recording, ~45 minutes, no stopping to re-do an answer.** Real rounds do not let you re-take. The re-take happens in review, not in the room.

## Expected output

What the round should look like when it is over — this is the shape to compare
your own numbers against, not a score to fake:

```text
questions answered      6-8
recording length        ~45 min, one file, unbroken
per-answer length       75-120s, Action started within the first 20s
rubric, per answer      0-2 on each of 5 signals, max 10

8+/10 average           interview-ready
6-7/10 average          a few categories need a sharper story or a tighter budget
below 6/10              re-drill the weak categories before Mock #3
```

The average is less interesting than the spread. A round that scores 9, 9, 9, 3,
9 has one broken story and is a much easier fix than a round that scores 6
across the board, which is a delivery problem rather than a content one.

## Steps

1. Set up: partner if you can find one, solo if not. Both are described above.
2. Put the questions somewhere you cannot read ahead — a partner reading them, or
   text-to-speech, or a folded sheet.
3. Hit record once. Run the whole forty-five minutes.
4. Stop. Do not review it the same hour; you will be too close to it.
5. Review pass one, content. Score every answer on the five signals.
6. Review pass two, delivery. Ignore content entirely.
7. Write up both passes: three content fixes, three delivery fixes.
8. Re-record your two weakest answers with the fixes applied.
9. Note any coverage gap and carry it into the
   [mini-project](../mini-project/README.md).

## The Solution

There is no single right answer to a round, so the worked answer here is the
**review protocol** — the part everyone skips, and the part that produces all the
improvement.

Same protocol as the Phase-1 mock recording. Watch your recording **twice, with two different lenses.**

**Pass 1 — Content (sound off if you want).** For each answer, score the five signals on the rubric above. Specifically catch:

- Where did the "I" disappear into "we"?
- Which answers ended without a number?
- Which answers buried the result under a long Situation?
- Did you tell any story twice (a coverage gap)?

**Pass 2 — Delivery (watch/listen at 1×).** Ignore content; watch *how* you came across:

- Pace — too fast (nervous) or too slow (rambling)?
- Filler words — count the "ums," "likes," "kind ofs." Everyone has some; note the rate.
- Energy — did you sound engaged or flat? The proudest-work answer especially should have energy.
- If on video: eye contact, posture, whether you looked like you were reading.

Write up both passes in `behavioral/mock-rounds/round-01.md`: the per-answer rubric scores, your three biggest content fixes, and your three biggest delivery fixes. Then **re-record your two weakest answers** with the fixes applied.

### What a worked review entry looks like

This is one answer's entry from a real-shaped review, written out in full so you
can see the level of specificity that makes a review useful:

> **Q4 — Failure. Score: ownership 2, impact 1, self-awareness 2, collaboration
> 1, growth 2. Total 8/10. Length 1:52 — too long.**
>
> *Content.* The mistake is owned cleanly and early, which is the hard part and
> it is done. Two problems. First, the impact is soft: "some data had to be
> re-imported" — how much, and for how many customers? I know both numbers and
> did not say either. Second, the containment is buried; I spend forty seconds on
> what went wrong and twelve on what I did about it, which is backwards for this
> category. Fix: state the number in the Situation, and move thirty seconds from
> the mistake to the response.
>
> *Delivery.* I speed up markedly at 0:48, exactly where the mistake gets
> described, and my voice drops. It sounds like flinching even though the words
> do not flinch. Fix: slow down deliberately through that sentence — the content
> is already fine, so the only job is to not undercut it.
>
> *Re-record?* Yes. This is one of my two weakest.

Two things make that entry useful and both are easy to skip. It names a **fix**
rather than a fault, and the fix is small enough to actually apply before the
next take. "Be more confident" is not a fix. "Slow down through the sentence at
0:48" is.

## How to deliver it

Record however you like — phone, laptop, whatever will run for forty-five minutes
without stopping. Video is better than audio if you can, because pass two catches
things audio cannot.

Commit the notes and link the recordings:

## Common bugs to catch

- **Stopping to re-do an answer.** Symptom: a recording that sounds better than
  you are. The endurance is the exercise.
- **Reviewing it straight away.** Symptom: a review that is either defensive or
  brutal, and useful either way only by accident. Leave it a few hours.
- **One pass instead of two.** Symptom: you notice the filler words and miss the
  missing numbers, or the reverse. The lenses do not work at the same time.
- **Scoring generously.** Symptom: an 8.5 average and a round you would not want
  a stranger to hear. Score it as if it were somebody else's.
- **Fixes that are not actions.** Symptom: "be more concise" written three times
  in the notes and nothing changing. Name the sentence, name the cut.
- **Skipping the re-records.** Symptom: a full review and no improvement. The
  re-record is where the review becomes a change.
- **Telling one story twice and letting it go.** Symptom: a coverage hole that
  surfaces for the first time in Mock #3, under a real clock.

## Stretch

- Interview somebody else through the same eight questions. Hearing a missing "I"
  in another person's answer is the fastest way to start hearing it in your own.
- Run the round again a week later with the questions in a **different order**.
  The opener and the closer are the two most rehearsed answers, and moving them
  shows how much of your fluency is order-dependent.
- Add a follow-up to every answer, chosen to be the one you least want. Doing
  that once makes [Challenge 2](./challenge-02-hostile-and-curveball-round.md)
  much less unpleasant.
