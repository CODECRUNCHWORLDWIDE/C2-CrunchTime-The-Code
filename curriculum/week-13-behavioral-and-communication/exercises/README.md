# Week 13 — Exercises

Three behavioral drills, one per high-frequency category. These are **not coding problems** — they are STAR drafting drills. For each, you draft a written STAR answer to a real prompt from your own experience, time the spoken version to under two minutes, and revise against the rubric. Draft each on your own before consulting [SOLUTIONS.md](./SOLUTIONS.md), which holds exemplar answers and a per-exercise rubric.

| # | File | Category | Deliverable | Target time |
|---|------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| 1 | [exercise-01-conflict-star.md](./exercise-01-conflict-star.md) | Conflict / disagreement | A STAR answer to a conflict prompt, under 2:00 spoken | 30 min |
| 2 | [exercise-02-failure-star.md](./exercise-02-failure-star.md) | Failure / mistake | A STAR answer that lands a real lesson, under 2:00 spoken | 30 min |
| 3 | [exercise-03-leadership-star.md](./exercise-03-leadership-star.md) | Leadership / initiative | A STAR answer with a quantified result, under 2:00 spoken | 30 min |

**How to use:**

1. Open the file. Read the prompt and the STAR checklist embedded at the top.
2. Close the lecture notes. Draft your answer in STAR — Situation, Task, Action, Result — from a *real* experience of your own.
3. Read it aloud at full volume and **time it**. If it runs over 2:00, cut the Situation first, then trim the Action to its three strongest steps. The timer is your self-test block: a story that "feels like two minutes" is usually three.
4. Record the spoken version if you can. Listen back. Note where you drifted into "we," where the Result failed to land, where you over-built the setup.
5. Read the corresponding section of [SOLUTIONS.md](./SOLUTIONS.md), compare against the exemplar and the rubric, and revise. Note which STAR section you under-developed.

Each story you draft here is also a candidate for your mini-project story bank — do not throw these away. A polished conflict, failure, and leadership story are three of the twelve slots already.

Solve order: 1, 2, 3 (conflict, failure, leadership — the three most-asked categories). The exercises increase in Result-quantification density; by Exercise 3 you should be landing a numeric or concretely-observable Result without prompting.
