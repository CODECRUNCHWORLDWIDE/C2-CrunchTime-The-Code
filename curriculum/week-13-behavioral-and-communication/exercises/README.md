# Week 13 — Exercises

Five STAR drafting drills. Each drill walks you through writing, refining, and recording one behavioral story in the four-part STAR structure. Unlike the algorithm-week drills, the deliverable is not code — it is a **rehearsed, recorded story** committed to your portfolio's `behavioral/story-bank/` folder. By Sunday these five join the four rough drafts from your Week 1 and Week 4 homework to form the twelve-plus-story bank the mini-project ships.

| 1 | [exercise-01-debugging-story.md](./exercise-01-debugging-story.md) | "Tell me about a time you debugged a hard problem." | Accomplishment / Ambiguity | Easy (warm-up) |
| 2 | [exercise-02-conflict-story.md](./exercise-02-conflict-story.md) | "Tell me about a disagreement with a coworker." | Conflict | Medium |

| 4 | [exercise-04-failure-story.md](./exercise-04-failure-story.md) | "Tell me about a time you failed." | Failure / mistake | Hard |
| 5 | [exercise-05-ambiguity-story.md](./exercise-05-ambiguity-story.md) | "Tell me about a time the requirements were unclear." | Ambiguity | Medium |

Exercise 1 is the warm-up — a debugging story is the easiest to make concrete because the technical detail comes naturally. Drills 2 and 4 (conflict, failure) are the ones most candidates handle worst, so we drill them in the middle of the week with full worked examples. Exercise 5 (ambiguity) is the one whose Action beats most resemble the FRAME Frame step.

## How to work each drill

Each drill file contains a full worked example — a believable, specific STAR answer written out in ~250–350 words. **Read the worked example after you draft your own, not before.** The point of the exercise is to surface *your* story; reading the model first will anchor you to ours.

The protocol for every drill:

1. **Draft** your own answer to the prompt using [`star_template.md`](./star_template.md). Use a real experience. If you genuinely lack one for a category, use the closest real situation and note the gap.
2. **Time and tighten** to the 10/10/60/20 budget. Cut the Situation; expand the Action.
3. **Quantify** the Result. Find the number. If you have no hard number, use an honest estimate and label it.
4. **Record** yourself delivering it from memory (audio or video).
5. **Listen back** at 1.25–1.5×. Note where you over-spent on setup, where the "I" went missing, and whether you landed a number.
6. **Re-record** the tightened version. The second take is always better.
7. **Read the worked example** in the drill file, compare, and steal any structural move that sharpens yours.
8. **Commit** the write-up and a note on the recording to your portfolio.

## A note on recording and listening back

This is the part that works and the part everyone wants to skip. You cannot hear your own filler words, your own buried results, or your own missing "I" until you play yourself back — exactly as you could not feel your own FRAME gaps until you recorded a coding solve in Phase 1. Listen at 1.25× or 1.5×; the speed-up makes rambling painfully obvious. One honest listen-back is worth three silent re-reads. Protect this hour.
