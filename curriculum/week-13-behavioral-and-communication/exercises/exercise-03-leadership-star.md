# Exercise 3 — Leadership / Initiative (STAR)

> **Category:** Leadership / initiative. **Target time:** 30 min to draft and revise; the spoken answer must come in under 2:00.

## STAR checklist (read before drafting)

- [ ] **Situation** is one to two sentences and shows there was *no one assigned* to do this — you stepped in.
- [ ] **Task** makes clear you took this on *yourself*; it was not handed to you.
- [ ] **Action** is first-person and shows you creating motion that would not have happened otherwise.
- [ ] **Result** is **quantified** (a number) or a **concrete observable** — this exercise specifically drills the quantified Result.
- [ ] You do **not** need a manager title. Driving a decision, unblocking a teammate, or filling a gap all count.
- [ ] Spoken aloud, it comes in under **2:00**. You timed it.

---

## The prompt

> **"Tell me about a time you took the lead on something or showed initiative. What did you do, and what was the outcome?"**

The canonical Leadership/initiative prompt. Variants: "a time you stepped up," "you drove a project," "no one was assigned and you took it on," "you went beyond your role." All one category.

## What this question scores

The competency is **agency** — do you move things forward without being told to? The interviewer is looking for evidence that, dropped into a team, you will notice what needs doing and do it, rather than waiting for assignment. Crucially, this is *not* a question about having held a formal leadership role. Most strong answers come from people with no title at all.

## The trap to avoid

Waiting for a "real" leadership story — a team you managed, a title you held — and concluding you have none. That is the wrong filter. Leadership in this question means *initiative*: a design discussion you drove to a decision, a stuck teammate you unblocked, a gap you noticed and filled, a process you fixed because it annoyed you and everyone else. The strong story is any moment you created motion that would not have existed without you. The discriminator from a teamwork story: here, *you* are the engine.

## This exercise's extra requirement: quantify the Result

Exercises 1 and 2 asked for a landed Result. This one specifically drills **quantification**. Your Result must carry a number or a concrete observable:

- **Metric:** "cut the team's build time from 9 minutes to 90 seconds," "the runbook I wrote dropped on-call escalations from ~4 a week to near zero."
- **Concrete observable (when no honest number exists):** "the design doc I drove became the template the team used for the next three projects," "the teammate I unblocked shipped on the original date."

Do **not** fabricate a number. If you do not have the measurement, use a concrete observable. The bar is specificity, not false precision.
