# Week 13 — Category-Recognition Quiz

Ten behavioral prompts. **Do not answer them.** For each, decide three things, fast:

1. Which of the **eight categories** does it test? (Conflict / Failure / Leadership / Ambiguity / Teamwork / Difficult-person / Accomplishment / Why-role. Some are hybrids — name the hybrid.)
2. Which **story from your bank** would you deploy?
3. What is the **top signal** the interviewer wants from this prompt?

This is the Research constraints step, applied to behavioral. Time yourself — 15 seconds per question is the target. Lectures closed.

Answer key at the bottom. (Story choices in the key are illustrative — yours will differ; what's graded is whether the *category* and *top signal* are right.)

---

**Q1.** "Tell me about the project you're proudest of."

**Q2.** "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision your manager had already made."

**Q3.** "Describe a time you had to deliver something but the requirements kept changing."

**Q4.** "Tell me about a time you made a mistake that had real consequences."

**Q5.** "Tell me about a time you got a group of people to adopt something, even though it wasn't your job to make them."

**Q6.** "Tell me about a teammate who was hard to work with, and how you handled it."

**Q7.** "Walk me through your background — what brought you here?"

**Q8.** "Tell me about a time you and a peer wanted to take a project in two different directions, and you eventually brought the team along to one of them."

**Q9.** "Tell me about a time a project you owned went off the rails, and what you did about it."

**Q10.** "Why this team specifically, and not somewhere else?"

## Answer key

<details>
<summary>Click after attempting all ten</summary>

1. **Accomplishment.** Top signal: **impact** (and ownership). Deploy your strongest-*number* story — debugging or checkout-latency. The whole point of "proudest" is to let you lead with a quantified, owned result, so pick the story with the cleanest metric.

2. **Conflict — with a Leadership tinge** (you disagreed *and* a decision was already made, so resolving it productively is the work). Top signal: **collaboration + self-awareness**. Deploy your conflict story (the DB-choice disagreement). Lead the Action with how you sought to understand their reasoning; land the relationship outcome.

3. **Ambiguity.** Top signal: **ownership + impact**. Deploy your ambiguity story (the under-specified dashboard). The tell is "requirements kept changing" — surface the explicit assumption and the cheap validation, then the ship.

4. **Failure.** Top signal: **ownership + growth**. Deploy your failure story (the migration outage). The phrase "real consequences" is doing work — they want a genuine failure, not a near-miss. Own it; name the change.

5. **Leadership / influence-without-authority.** Top signal: **ownership + collaboration**. Deploy your leadership story (the flaky-CI rotation). The phrase "wasn't your job to make them" is the influence tell — the proof is that it stuck without you.

6. **Difficult person.** Top signal: **self-awareness + collaboration** (empathy, not blame). Deploy a difficult-person story (the teammate who went dark). The interviewer grades *you*, not the difficult person — show empathy and a de-escalation, no contempt.

7. **Why-this-role / "tell me about yourself."** Top signal: **communication + fit**. Deploy your 90-second pitch. A narrative with a through-line, not a chronological résumé recitation.

8. **Conflict + Leadership hybrid.** Top signal: **collaboration + ownership**. Deploy your cross-team-alignment story. Naming it as a hybrid ("this is really conflict *and* influence") and picking a story that lands both beats — the disagreement *and* bringing people along — is itself the senior signal.

9. **Failure** (with possible Leadership/Ownership flavor in "what you did about it"). Top signal: **ownership + growth**. Deploy a failure or incident story. The "what you did about it" half wants the containment and the lesson, so don't stop at the failure — show the response.

10. **Why-this-role / Why-us.** Top signal: **fit + motivation**. This is *not* a story prompt — it's the genuine-motivation check. Name something specific and true about *this* team. A generic "I want impact" answer that fits any company scores zero.

</details>

## How to score

| Score | Meaning |
|------:|---------|
| 9–10 | Category recognition is interview-ready, including the hybrids (Q2, Q8) and the non-story prompts (Q7, Q10). Your story selection is fast. Move on to assembling the bank. |
| 7–8 | Good — most learners miss the hybrids (Q2, Q8) or mis-tag Q9 as Leadership when it's primarily Failure. Re-read [Lecture 1 §2–3](./lecture-notes/01-the-eight-categories-and-the-rubric.md) on the categories and the hybrids. |
| 5–6 | The categories aren't automatic yet, or you have coverage gaps (a category where you couldn't name a story). Re-read Lecture 1, then check your coverage matrix — an empty column shows up here as "I don't have a story for that." |
| <5 | Recognition isn't reflexive. Re-read all three lectures, do the five drills, build the coverage matrix, then retake. |

This quiz is about **fluency**, not difficulty. The discriminating questions are Q2 and Q8 (hybrids — naming both categories is the senior move), Q7 and Q10 (the non-story "why" prompts that need a pitch and genuine motivation, not a STAR story), and Q9 (primarily Failure, not Leadership, despite "what you did about it"). The most-missed are the hybrids: candidates pick one category and miss that a story landing *both* beats scores higher.
