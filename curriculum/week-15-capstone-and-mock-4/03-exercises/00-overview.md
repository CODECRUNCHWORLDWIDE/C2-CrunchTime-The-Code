# Week 15 — Exercises

Three exercises drilling the capstone synthesis skills. This is the course's final week, so the exercises are **portfolio-build and recruiter-prep tasks**, not algorithm problems — you have spent 14 weeks on the algorithms; this week you make them *visible* and you make *yourself* findable. Each exercise produces a real artifact you keep and ship as part of the capstone.

| # | File | Skill | Produces | Target time |
|---|------|-------|----------|-------------|
| 1 | [exercise-01-portfolio-readme.md](./exercise-01-portfolio-readme.md) | Curate the public portfolio | The portfolio README — who/what/start-here + index by pattern | 90 min |
| 2 | [exercise-02-resume-rewrite.md](./exercise-02-resume-rewrite.md) | The resume bullet shape | Five bullets rewritten into action-verb + scope + impact | 60 min |
| 3 | [exercise-03-linkedin-audit.md](./exercise-03-linkedin-audit.md) | LinkedIn for recruiter search | The four levers rewritten (headline, About, Featured, skills) | 60 min |

**How to use:**

1. Open the exercise file. Read the spec and the acceptance criteria.
2. Do the work against *your own* materials — your real write-ups, your real resume, your real LinkedIn. These are not hypothetical drills; the output is part of your capstone.
3. Check your output against the acceptance criteria in the file.
4. Read the corresponding section of [SOLUTIONS.md](./SOLUTIONS.md) — it has worked before/after examples — and compare. Note what you under-did.

Solve order: 1, 2, 3 (portfolio first because it is the capstone's spine and the thing the resume and LinkedIn will link to; resume and LinkedIn after, since both point back to the portfolio).

Unlike the algorithm weeks, there is no `SOLUTIONS` with a single correct answer — a resume bullet has many right forms. SOLUTIONS gives worked *examples* and the rubric each artifact is graded on, not an answer key. The grading is whether your artifact meets the acceptance criteria, which a peer or mentor can check in five minutes.
