# Exercise 2 — Rewrite Five Resume Bullets

**Skill:** The resume bullet shape (Lecture 3 §1).
**Produces:** five bullets from your real resume rewritten into the canonical shape.
**Target time:** 60 minutes.

## The task

Take five bullets from your current resume and rewrite each into the canonical shape: **action verb + what you built + measurable scope or impact**. Then add the sixth bullet — the projects-section bullet that links your portfolio. By the end you have six strong bullets you can drop straight into the one-page resume.

If your current resume is thin (career-changer, new grad), pull bullets from any source of real work: a job, an internship, a side project, a class project, the CrunchTime portfolio itself. The shape is the same regardless of source.

## Specification

For each of five bullets:

1. **Identify the weak shape.** Most weak bullets start with "Responsible for," "Helped with," "Worked on," "Assisted," or describe a duty rather than an achievement.
2. **Rewrite into the strong shape:** a strong action verb, the specific thing you built or changed, and a measurable scope or impact. Quantify if you can; name the scope if you cannot.
3. **Cut the banned phrases entirely.** "Responsible for," "helped," "worked on," "familiar with" do not appear in any rewritten bullet.

Then write the **portfolio projects bullet** (the sixth), in the strong shape, linking the portfolio.

## Worked transformation (one example)

**Before:** "Responsible for maintaining the company's reporting database."

**Diagnosis:** "Responsible for" describes presence, not achievement. No verb of action, no scope, no impact.

**After:** "Owned and optimized a 2TB reporting Postgres instance serving 50+ daily analyst queries; cut the slowest report's runtime from 4 minutes to 8 seconds by adding three composite indexes."

The strong version has a verb (owned, optimized), the thing (a 2TB reporting database), the scope (50+ daily queries), and a quantified impact (4 min → 8 sec).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **Five bullets rewritten**, each in action-verb + what-you-built + measurable-scope-or-impact shape.
- [ ] **Zero banned phrases** across all five ("responsible for," "helped," "worked on," "assisted," "familiar with").
- [ ] **Every bullet starts with a strong action verb** (designed, built, shipped, cut, rebuilt, owned, automated, led, reduced, scaled — not "was," "did," "had").
- [ ] **At least three of the five are quantified** with a number (percentage, count, time, scale). The other two name a concrete scope.
- [ ] **The sixth bullet links the portfolio** and is itself in the strong shape.
- [ ] All six together would fit comfortably on a one-page resume.

## Self-check

- Read each rewritten bullet aloud. Does it describe something you *achieved*, or something you were merely *present for*? If present-for, it is not done.
- Cover the company name. Can a reader still tell what you accomplished? A bullet that only makes sense with the company name attached is too vague.
- Count the numbers. Three of five quantified is the floor; aim higher.

## Why this exercise

The bullet shape is the entire resume skill, and it is the one most candidates get wrong. A recruiter's six-second skim rewards strong bullets and punishes weak ones with equal speed. Drilling five real bullets here makes the shape reflexive, so when you write the seventh and eighth for the actual resume they come out strong the first time.
