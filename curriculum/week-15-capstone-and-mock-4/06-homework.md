# Week 15 — Homework

The course's final homework: the **50-application sprint plan** plus the **behavioral-story polish**. Allow ~5 hours. Unlike the algorithm weeks, this homework produces a *plan you execute over the coming weeks*, not problems you solve today — the course ends, the job search begins, and this is the bridge.

The two tasks: build the application sprint plan (the cadence, the tracking, the funnel targets) that you will run for the next four to six weeks; and polish the three-to-five behavioral stories so they are loaded and STAR-shaped for every interview. By Sunday the sprint plan is committed and the first application is sent.

---

## Part 1 — The 50-application sprint plan (~3 hours)

### Spec

Build a concrete, week-by-week plan to put ~50 applications in flight over the coming weeks, weighted toward warm referrals, with a tracking spreadsheet and funnel targets. This runs on the target list from [Challenge 1](./04-challenges/challenge-01-target-list-and-outreach.md).

Your plan must contain:

1. **The cadence.** How many applications per week, and on which days. The default: ~10–12/week over 4–5 weeks, with a fixed "application block" (e.g., Tuesday and Thursday mornings) so it does not slip.
2. **The warm-referral tilt.** For each company on the target list, note whether you have a warm path (a connection, an alum, a meetup contact). The plan prioritizes warm paths first — they convert an order of magnitude better — and only goes cold where no warm path exists.
3. **The tracking spreadsheet.** Columns: company, tier, role, date applied, warm/cold, recruiter contact, status (applied / replied / phone screen / onsite / offer / rejected), next action, notes. Start it now with your target-list companies as rows.
4. **The funnel targets.** Using the funnel math (resources), state the rough expectation: from ~50 applications, expect ~5–6 replies, ~3 phone screens, ~1 onsite. Name the number of *warm* applications you will aim for, since warm collapses the funnel.
5. **The weekly review ritual.** A 30-minute weekly block to update the tracker, send follow-ups (once each, 5–7 business days after no reply), and diagnose the bottleneck (top = outreach/targeting; middle = phone-screen prep; bottom = interview performance).

### Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A **week-by-week cadence** that puts ~50 applications in flight over 4–6 weeks, with fixed application blocks.
- [ ] A **warm-referral tilt** — warm paths identified and prioritized over cold.
- [ ] A **tracking spreadsheet** started, with the funnel columns and the target-list companies as rows.
- [ ] **Funnel targets** stated, with a specific warm-application goal.
- [ ] A **weekly review ritual** defined (tracker update + follow-ups + bottleneck diagnosis).
- [ ] The **first application sent** by Sunday.

## Part 2 — Behavioral-story polish (~2 hours)

### Spec

You built 3–5 behavioral stories in Week 14. Polish them to interview-ready: each in tight STAR shape, each with a specific (ideally quantified) Result, each mapped to the common question types it answers.

For each story:

1. **STAR-shape it.** Situation (~15 sec), Task (~10 sec), Action (~30 sec), Result (~15 sec). Total ~70 seconds spoken. Trim anything that does not advance the arc.
2. **Sharpen the Result.** This is the highest-leverage edit. The Result must be specific — a number, an outcome, a lasting change. "It worked out" is not a Result.
3. **Map it to question types.** Tag each story with the questions it answers: "tell me about a conflict," "a hard bug," "a time you led," "a failure," "why this company." A story that maps to three question types is more valuable than one that maps to one.
4. **Rehearse each aloud once, timed.** Aim for ~70 seconds. Over two minutes means it needs trimming.

### Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **3–5 stories**, each in tight STAR shape, each ~70 seconds spoken.
- [ ] **Every Result is specific** — a number, an outcome, or a named lasting change.
- [ ] **Each story mapped** to the question types it answers; the set covers conflict, a hard technical problem, leadership/initiative, and a failure/learning.
- [ ] **Each rehearsed aloud once, timed.**

## Rubric

| Dimension | Weight | What "yes" looks like |
|-----------|-------:|----------------------|
| Sprint cadence + tracking | 30% | Week-by-week plan; spreadsheet started with funnel columns and target-list rows |
| Warm-referral tilt | 20% | Warm paths identified and prioritized; a specific warm-application goal |
| Funnel literacy | 15% | Targets stated from the funnel math; weekly bottleneck-diagnosis ritual |
| Behavioral STAR shape | 20% | 3–5 stories, tight STAR, ~70 sec each |
| Behavioral Results | 15% | Every Result specific/quantified; stories mapped to question types |

The sprint-cadence weight is the highest because a plan that does not get executed is worthless. The single most common post-course failure is *the sprint never starts* — the candidate finishes the portfolio, feels done, and never sends the applications. The fixed application blocks and the first-application-by-Sunday requirement exist to break that pattern.

---

## Suggested order

1. **Start the tracking spreadsheet first** — it makes the rest concrete. Drop your target-list companies in as rows.
2. **Identify warm paths** for each — this is where the leverage is.
3. **Set the cadence** and the weekly review ritual.
4. **State the funnel targets.**
5. **Then** polish the behavioral stories — sharpen the Results, map to question types, rehearse.
6. **Send the first application** before Sunday is over.

If time runs out, protect two things: the **tracking spreadsheet started** (so the sprint has a spine) and the **first application sent** (so the sprint actually begins). The behavioral polish can be finished in the first week of the sprint if needed.

## Acceptance

The course's homework — and the structured part of the program — is complete when:

- The **50-application sprint plan** is committed to `recruiter-prep/sprint-plan.md`.
- The **tracking spreadsheet** is started, with the target-list companies as rows.
- The **3–5 behavioral stories** are polished, STAR-shaped, and committed to `behavioral/`.
- The **first application is sent.**
- The quiz is taken and scored; the recruiter-readiness score is in the course retrospective.

The retrospective is the single most useful artifact to close the course. The thread most candidates underestimate is *execution*: the portfolio and the pack are the easy part because they are bounded; the sprint is the hard part because it is open-ended and requires showing up every week. Name, in the retrospective, the one habit that will keep the sprint running when the rejections start coming — because they will, and the candidates who get the offer are the ones who kept applying through them.

This is the end of the curriculum. The next thing you do is not homework — it is sending the second application, then the third. Go.
