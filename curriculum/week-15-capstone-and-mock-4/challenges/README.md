# Week 15 — Challenges

Two challenges. Both are required this week — Challenge 1 is the keystone of the capstone week (and of the whole program's mock sequence), and Challenge 2 is the system-design round that a junior loop now includes.

| # | Challenge | Format | Difficulty | Target time |
|---|-----------|--------|------------|-------------|
| 1 | [Mock #4 — Full Loop](./challenge-01-mock-4-full-loop.md) | A recorded simulated onsite loop under full real conditions: coding + system design + behavioral | — (a loop, not a problem) | ~2 h loop + ~2 h review/write-up |
| 2 | [System-Design Mock](./challenge-02-system-design-mock.md) | A 45-minute junior-level system-design mock round | Junior design | 45 min + write-up |

Challenge 1 is the week. It is not an algorithm problem — it is the protocol for running Mock #4 as a recorded full loop under *full real-interview conditions*: a stranger interviewer if obtainable, dressed as if real, no notes, a hard stop, and the full sequence of a coding round, a system-design round, and a behavioral round. The deliverable is the recording, the two-pass self-feedback, and the **closing trajectory across Mocks #1 → #4** — the single most predictive artifact in the portfolio.

Challenge 2 is the system-design round on its own, run as a focused 45-minute mock. It can be folded into Challenge 1's loop as the design round, or run separately as a dedicated rep — at the junior level, the design round is the one most candidates have practiced least, so a standalone rep is worth it.

## What a complete challenge looks like

**Challenge 1 (the mock loop)** — the deliverable is the recording link, the immediate notes, the pass-1 timestamps, and the self-feedback write-up with the four-mock trajectory and the one weakness carried forward. See the challenge file for the exact artifact list, the full-loop sequence, and the fallback coding problem.

**Challenge 2 (the design mock)** — the deliverable is a written design (from one of the prompts: pastebin / rate limiter / news feed at small scale) following the requirements → estimation → high-level design → deep-dive → trade-offs framework, plus a recording of you talking through it, and a self-grade against the rubric.

## A note on the mock as the keystone of the program

Mock #4 is the highest-signal artifact you produce this week, and the close of the four-mock arc that has run since Week 4. Three reasons it matters more than any single drill:

1. **It is the full-conditions dress rehearsal.** The next loop after Mock #4 is a real one, for a job. Mock #4 is where you discover any failure mode under full conditions — stranger interviewer, dressed as if real, no notes, hard stop, the full coding + design + behavioral loop — at low stakes, before it costs you a real offer.
2. **The four-mock trajectory is what a senior reads.** Mock #1 → #2 → #3 → #4 is a complete record of whether you can self-correct over time — the single most predictive trait of someone who will grow on the job. The closing trajectory section is where you prove it, and where the one weakness you carry forward seeds your personalized study plan.
3. **The design round is now part of the loop.** A junior onsite includes system design. Challenge 2 (and Exercise 2) make sure the design round is not the round that sinks an otherwise-strong loop.

If anything must slip this week, slip the second system-design write-up or the badges polish — **do not slip Mock #4.** The mock and the published capstone are the two things this week exists to produce.
