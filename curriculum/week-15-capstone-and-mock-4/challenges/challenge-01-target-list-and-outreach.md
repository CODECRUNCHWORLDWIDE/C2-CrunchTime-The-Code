# Challenge 1 — The Target-Company List and the Outreach Trio

**Skill:** the target list (Lecture 3 §3) and the outreach template (Lecture 3 §4).
**Produces:** a tiered list of 25–40 companies plus the three outreach templates.
**Target time:** 120 minutes.
**Required.**

## The task

Build the spine of your job search: a target-company list of 25–40 companies, tiered into reach / match / safety, each with a named team or product and a "why this one" sentence — and the outreach template trio (cold recruiter, warm referral, follow-up) ready to send. This challenge has the deepest Match step of the week: choosing the *right* companies and the *right* tiering is the strategic decision the whole search runs on.

## Part A — the target list (75 minutes)

### Spec

Produce a table of 25–40 companies. For each:

- **Tier:** reach / match / safety, defaulting to the **20/60/20** split (~6–8 reach, ~18–24 match, ~6–8 safety for a list of ~35).
- **Team or product:** named where you can. "Stripe — payments reliability," not just "Stripe." If you genuinely cannot name a team, leave it blank but note it as a gap to research.
- **Role link:** the actual job posting URL, where one exists.
- **Why this one:** one true, specific sentence. If you cannot write a true one, the company does not belong on the list — cut it.

### How to source the companies

- Start from where you already are: companies whose products you use, whose engineering blogs you read, whose people you have met.
- Expand with the obvious lists (the big tech companies, the well-known startups in your domain) but do not let them dominate — those are mostly reach tier and a list that is all reach produces no interviews.
- Fill the match tier deliberately: mid-size companies where your profile clearly fits the bar. This is 60% of the list and where most of your real interviews come from.
- Fill the safety tier honestly: companies where you are above the bar. Not "settling" — these keep the funnel full and give you an offer in hand for leverage.

### Acceptance criteria (Part A)

- [ ] **25–40 companies**, no fewer and no more (more stops being targeted; fewer starves the funnel).
- [ ] **Tiered roughly 20/60/20**; the split is defensible if a mentor asks.
- [ ] **A named team or product** for the majority of rows (blanks flagged as research gaps).
- [ ] **A true, specific "why this one"** for every row — no generic "great culture" filler.
- [ ] No row you could not honestly explain to the company's own engineer.

## Part B — the outreach trio (45 minutes)

### Spec

Write three reusable templates, each with the placeholders marked, ready to fill per-company:

1. **Cold recruiter** — 4–6 sentences; references something real about the team; names the role; one specific achievement; portfolio link; asks only for a 15-minute chat.
2. **Warm referral** — leads with the mutual connection; same length; same no-ask-yet discipline.
3. **Follow-up** — 2–3 sentences; sent once, 5–7 business days after no reply; gracious exit if no interest.

Templates are in [Lecture 3 §4](../lecture-notes/03-the-recruiter-prep-pack.md#4-the-outreach-template-trio); adapt them to your voice rather than copying verbatim — a message that reads as a template gets filed as spam.

### Then: fill one of each, for real

Pick one reach-tier and one match-tier company from your list. Fully fill the cold-recruiter template for each (real team, real "why this one," real achievement, real portfolio link). These two filled messages are what you send — the first by Sunday night.

### Acceptance criteria (Part B)

- [ ] **Three templates** written: cold recruiter, warm referral, follow-up.
- [ ] Each is **short** (cold and warm 4–6 sentences; follow-up 2–3), **specific** (references something real), and makes **no ask beyond a 15-minute chat**.
- [ ] Each **links the portfolio** once, in context.
- [ ] **Two messages fully filled** for two real companies, ready to send.
- [ ] The voice is **yours**, not the template's — it does not read as a form letter.

## How this challenge is graded

| Dimension | Weight | What "yes" looks like |
|-----------|-------:|----------------------|
| Targeting (list quality) | 35% | 25–40 companies, tiered 20/60/20, named teams, true "why this one" each |
| Outreach craft | 30% | Trio written; short, specific, no-ask-yet, portfolio linked; voice is yours |
| Strategic defense | 20% | The tiering and the company choices survive a mentor's "why these?" |
| Ready to send | 15% | Two real messages fully filled; first one sent by Sunday |

The targeting weight is the highest because it is the decision everything downstream depends on. A great outreach message to the wrong company is wasted; a list with no named teams produces generic outreach that gets ignored. Spend the time on Part A.

## Why this challenge

The target list is the strategic core of the job search and the most-skipped piece. Candidates love to polish the resume and dread the target list because it forces real choices — which companies, at what tier, and an honest "why." But the funnel math is unforgiving: an untargeted blast of 50 applications converts worse than 25 targeted ones with warm-referral outreach. This challenge front-loads the hard strategic work so the application sprint in the homework runs on a real plan instead of a spray.
