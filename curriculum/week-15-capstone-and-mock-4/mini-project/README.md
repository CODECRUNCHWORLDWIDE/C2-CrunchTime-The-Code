# Capstone — The Public Portfolio, Mock #4, and the Recruiter-Prep Pack

> The course's final deliverable, and the largest. This is not a mini-project — it is the **capstone**: the synthesis of fifteen weeks into three cross-linked artifacts a hiring manager can see and act on. A public portfolio anyone can scroll to see UMPIRE applied to 60+ problems; the recorded Mock #4 under full real-interview conditions; and the complete recruiter-prep pack (resume, LinkedIn, target list, outreach). By Sunday you finish the course not "studying for interviews" but running a real job search with a portfolio behind you.

**This README is the capstone spec.** The master guide — the canonical, course-wide version with the full rubric, the directory layout, and the submission process — lives at **[projects/capstone](../../projects/)**. This file scopes the Week-15 capstone and points there; read both. If `projects/capstone` is not yet populated in your copy of the course, this spec is authoritative and the master guide will mirror it.

**Estimated time:** 18 hours, split across the whole week (Monday audit through Sunday ship).

---

## Why this is the capstone, not a mini-project

Every prior week's mini-project graded *one or two write-ups as a pair*. The capstone grades the **whole arc** — fourteen weeks of accumulated reps, organized and made public, plus the exit measurement and the conversion layer. Three reasons it is scaled up:

1. **It is the artifact that gets you hired.** A pile of private write-ups produces zero offers. The capstone is the curation, the proof-under-pressure, and the outreach machine that turn fifteen weeks of work into onsite invitations. It is the highest-stakes deliverable of the course because it is the one with a real-world payoff.

2. **It synthesizes, it does not add.** There is no new pattern. The capstone takes what exists — your 60+ write-ups, your mock skill, your story — and makes it legible to a hiring manager. The work is curation, audit, measurement, and conversion, not problem-solving. That is a different and harder skill than another week of LeetCode.

3. **It is cross-linked, and the cross-links are graded.** The resume links the portfolio; LinkedIn links the portfolio; the outreach links the portfolio; the "why this one" feeds the outreach. The capstone is a *system*, and a system where the pieces do not connect is a pile of pieces. The cross-linking is part of the rubric.

## What you ship

Three artifacts, cross-linked, all public or pushed.

```
crunchtime-portfolio-<you>/   (public repo)
├── README.md                         ← the curated portfolio front door (Exercise 1)
├── umpire-writeups/
│   ├── c2-week-01/ ... c2-week-14/    ← the 60+ write-ups, audited public-ready
│   └── ...
├── mocks/
│   ├── mock-01/ ... mock-03/          ← prior mocks
│   └── mock-04/
│       ├── recording-link.md
│       ├── immediate-notes.md
│       ├── timestamps.md
│       └── self-feedback.md           ← the exit-mock verdict (Challenge 2)
├── recruiter-prep/
│   ├── resume.md (or resume.pdf link) ← one page, strong bullets (Exercise 2)
│   ├── linkedin.md                    ← the four levers drafted (Exercise 3)
│   ├── target-list.md                 ← 25–40 tiered companies (Challenge 1)
│   └── outreach-templates.md          ← the trio + two filled messages (Challenge 1)
└── course-retrospective.md            ← the arc, from week 1 to now
```

### Artifact 1 — the public portfolio

The curated README (Exercise 1) plus all 60+ write-ups audited to public-ready standard (Lecture 1 §4). Public repo, navigable in four minutes, indexed by pattern, three or four flagship pieces in "start here."

### Artifact 2 — the recorded Mock #4

The exit mock (Challenge 2): partner-run, recorded, both behavioral bookends, with the self-feedback note whose headline is the verdict on whether the three prior behavior changes stuck.

### Artifact 3 — the recruiter-prep pack

The four cross-linked pieces: the one-page resume (Exercise 2), the LinkedIn levers (Exercise 3), the tiered target list and outreach trio (Challenge 1). The pack converts the portfolio into interviews.

Plus the **course retrospective** — a 400-word reflection at `course-retrospective.md` on the arc from "could not name a pattern" to "ships UMPIRE on any prompt in 30 seconds."

## How the capstone is graded

| Dimension | Weight | What "yes" looks like |
|-----------|-------:|----------------------|
| Portfolio — curation | 20% | Public repo; README who/what/start-here above the fold; index by pattern; 60+ entries; 3–4 flagship pieces |
| Portfolio — public-ready audit | 15% | Write-ups audited: one-line summaries, no private asides, working code blocks, resolving links |
| Mock #4 | 20% | Recorded, partner-run, both bookends; self-feedback note with the three-change verdict table |
| Recruiter pack — resume | 10% | One page; every bullet in the strong shape; portfolio linked |
| Recruiter pack — LinkedIn | 5% | Four levers tuned for recruiter search; portfolio in Featured |
| Recruiter pack — target list + outreach | 15% | 25–40 companies tiered 20/60/20; outreach trio; two real messages ready |
| Cross-linking + retrospective | 15% | Resume/LinkedIn/outreach all link the portfolio; "why this one" feeds outreach; retrospective committed |

The cross-linking weight is the element unique to the capstone. The three artifacts must function as one system — resume to portfolio, LinkedIn to portfolio, outreach to portfolio. A capstone where the pieces exist but do not connect loses the 15%; a capstone where they connect reads as the work of someone who thinks in systems, which is the senior signal.

## The week's arc (how the pieces land across the days)

- **Monday** — portfolio audit; back-fill any missing write-ups so the count clears 60.
- **Tuesday** — build the portfolio README (Exercise 1); start the public-ready audit on the flagship pieces.
- **Wednesday** — resume (Exercise 2) and LinkedIn (Exercise 3); both link the portfolio.
- **Thursday** — target list + outreach (Challenge 1); Mock #4 setup.
- **Friday** — Mock #4, recorded (Challenge 2); immediate notes.
- **Saturday** — watch Mock #4, write the self-feedback verdict; finish the public-ready audit on the remaining write-ups.
- **Sunday** — write the course retrospective; final cross-link pass; flip the repo public; **send the first outreach message**; push.

## Acceptance

The capstone — and the course — is complete when:

- The **public portfolio repo** is public, with the curated README and 60+ audited write-ups indexed by pattern.
- **Mock #4** is recorded and the self-feedback note (with the three-change verdict) is committed.
- The **recruiter-prep pack** is complete: one-page resume, LinkedIn levers, tiered target list, outreach trio.
- All three artifacts are **cross-linked** — the portfolio is the hub.
- The **course retrospective** is committed.
- The first **outreach message is actually sent** by Sunday night.
- The push log shows daily commits Mon–Sun.

Total time budget: 18 hours across the week. If you exceed 22, stop and request a 1:1 with the Phase-3 lead — the over-budget usually means the portfolio audit is being done too perfectionistically (audit to "good," not "flawless") or the target list is being researched past the point of useful return.

## A note on finishing

This is the last thing you build in the course. It is tempting, at the end of fifteen weeks, to rush the capstone and call it done. Resist. The capstone is the only artifact a hiring manager will ever see — the lectures, the quizzes, the homework all served to produce *this*. Spend the time on the two non-negotiables: the **public portfolio** (the proof) and **Mock #4** (the proof-under-pressure). The recruiter pack converts them into interviews, but the portfolio and the mock are what the interviews are *about*.

When it is shipped and the first outreach is sent, you are done with the course and into the job search. The portfolio is behind you, four mocks are behind you, and the next mock you run is for real. Go get the offer — then come back and point the next cohort at your portfolio as the example of what "done" looks like.
