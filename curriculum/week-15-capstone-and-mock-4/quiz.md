# Week 15 — Recruiter-Readiness and Interview-Loop Self-Audit

Ten short prompts. Unlike the pattern-recognition quizzes of the algorithm weeks, this is a **self-audit**: each question tests whether you know the right move in a recruiter-prep or interview-loop situation. For each, decide the right answer before checking the key. Lectures closed. Target ~60 seconds per question.

Answer key at the bottom.

---

**Q1.** A hiring manager lands on your portfolio README. How long do you have, roughly, to convince them it is worth a closer look, and what three things must be visible before they scroll?

**Q2.** You have 64 write-ups in a private repo. Is that a portfolio? If not, what is missing?

**Q3.** Rewrite this resume bullet into the canonical shape: *"Responsible for helping maintain the team's test suite."*

**Q4.** A recruiter searches LinkedIn for "software engineer Python" in your metro and your profile does not appear. Which of the four levers is most likely the cause, and what is the fix?

**Q5.** Your target list is 30 companies, all of them FAANG and well-known unicorns. What is wrong with it, and what does the funnel predict will happen?

**Q6.** You send a cold outreach message to a recruiter and ask, in the first message, for a referral to the hiring manager. Why is this a mistake, and what should the first message ask for instead?

**Q7.** In Mock #4, you deliver a clean Match memo and solve the problem correctly, but you open with a three-minute chronological walk through your resume. Did you pass the exit mock? Why or why not?

**Q8.** Your Mock #4 self-feedback note says "I did better this time." What is wrong with that as a self-feedback finding, and what should the headline of the note be instead?

**Q9.** You have applied to 50 companies and gotten zero recruiter replies in three weeks. Where in the funnel is the bottleneck, and what are the two most likely fixes?

**Q10.** A behavioral-close answer ends with "...and it worked out fine in the end." What is missing, and why does it matter most?

## Answer key

<details>
<summary>Click after attempting all ten</summary>

1. **About four minutes total, but only ~20–30 seconds before they decide whether to give you the four minutes — so the who, the what, and the start-here must all be above the fold.** Lecture 1 §1–2. The README's first screenful carries the whole first impression.

2. **No — a private repo of files is raw material, not a portfolio.** Missing: it is not public (a hiring manager cannot see it), not curated (no "start here," no index by pattern), and not audited public-ready (private asides not stripped, code blocks not verified, one-line summaries absent). Lecture 1 §1, §4.

3. **Something like: "Cut the team's test-suite flakiness 60% and added 40+ regression cases, reducing CI false-failures from ~15/week to under 2."** Strong verb (cut/added/reduced), the thing (the test suite), quantified impact. The banned phrases "responsible for" and "helping maintain" are gone. Lecture 3 §1; Exercise 2.

4. **Most likely the headline (and/or skills), because recruiter search is literal and weights the headline heavily.** Fix: put the exact keywords ("Software Engineer," "Python") in the headline, repeat them in About, and order them in the top three skills. Synonyms ("scalable backends" instead of "Python") do not match the literal search. Lecture 3 §2.

5. **It is all reach tier — it violates the 20/60/20 split.** The funnel predicts very few replies, few or no onsites, and crushing morale, because your profile is below the typical bar at most of them. Fix: add match-tier (60%) and safety-tier (20%) companies where your profile fits or exceeds the bar. Lecture 3 §3.

6. **Asking for a referral in the first message is too big an ask before any relationship exists — it reads as transactional and gets ignored.** The first message asks only for a **15-minute chat**; the referral ask comes later, after the conversation. Lecture 3 §4.

7. **No — or at best a partial pass.** The exit mock tests the *whole slot*, including the behavioral open. A three-minute chronological resume-read with no thread is a fumbled open; in a real loop it costs you the first impression regardless of how clean the algorithm was. The fix: the 90-second narrative arc with a thread. Lecture 2 §1, §4.

8. **"I did better" is not a finding — it is not specific, not measured, and not testable.** The headline of the Mock #4 note should be the **three-change verdict table**: for each of the three behavior changes you have been practicing, did it hold under exit-mock pressure (Yes / Partial / No), with a timestamp as evidence. The change that cracked is what you carry into the real loop. Lecture 2 §3, §6.

9. **The bottleneck is at the top of the funnel — application → reply.** The two most likely fixes: (a) the outreach/targeting is weak — too cold, too generic, wrong companies; tilt toward warm referrals and named-team specificity; (b) the resume/LinkedIn are not converting the skim — weak bullets, missing keywords, no portfolio link. Lecture 3 §5; resources funnel table.

10. **The specific Result is missing.** STAR's Result is the most-graded part of a behavioral answer; "it worked out fine" conveys nothing. It must be specific and, where possible, quantified ("zero double-charges in the first month, and the check became the team's standard"). The Result is what the interviewer remembers and writes down. Lecture 2 §5.

</details>

## How to score

| Score | Meaning |
|------:|---------|
| 9-10 | Recruiter-ready. You know the right move in every loop and prep situation. Ship the capstone and start sending. |
| 7-8 | Good — re-read the lecture sections flagged on the questions you missed. The most-missed are usually Q4 (literal recruiter search), Q5 (tiering), and Q8 (the self-feedback verdict). |
| 5-6 | Re-read [Lecture 3](./lecture-notes/03-the-recruiter-prep-pack.md) (the pack) and [Lecture 2 §4–6](./lecture-notes/02-mock-4-the-exit-interview.md) (the bookends and the verdict). The conversion layer and the exit-mock measurement need another pass before you send applications. |
| <5 | The recruiter-prep model is not yet internalized. Re-read all three lectures, redo the exercises against your real materials, and retake. Do not start the application sprint until this is solid — an untargeted sprint on a weak pack wastes the funnel. |

This quiz is about **judgment**, not difficulty. The discriminating questions are Q5 (an all-reach target list is the most common strategic error), Q6 (the no-ask-yet outreach discipline), and Q8 (the self-feedback verdict — measurement, not vibes). Most candidates miss Q5 first; over-targeting reach companies is the classic ambitious-candidate mistake.

When done, the [homework](./homework.md) — the 50-application sprint plan — is next.
