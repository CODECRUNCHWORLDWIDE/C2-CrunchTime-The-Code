## On the portfolio

The portfolio is a *product*, and the user is a hiring manager with four minutes.

- **A portfolio is curated, not complete.** Sixty write-ups is the raw material; the portfolio is the curated index plus three or four flagship pieces the reader should start with. The "start here" set is the highest-leverage element — it tells a skimmer where your best work is.
- **The README is the whole UX.** A reader who lands on the repo sees the README first. It must state, above the fold: who you are (one line), what the repo is (one line), and where to start (three or four links). Everything else is below the fold.
- **Group by pattern, not by week.** A hiring manager does not care that something was "Week 6." They care that you can do BFS, DFS, DP, backtracking. Index by the 14 patterns; mention the week only as a secondary tag.
- **Every entry has the same six sections.** Understand, Match, Plan, Implement, Review, Evaluate — in that order, every time. Consistency is itself a signal: it says "this person runs a process."
- **Public-ready is different from drill-ready.** A drill note can say "I was stuck for 10 minutes here." A public entry cannot. The audit (Lecture 1 §4) strips the asides, fixes the links, and verifies the code runs.

## On the resume

The one-page constraint forces ruthless editing. The bullet shape is the work.

| Weak shape | Strong shape |
|------------|--------------|
| "Responsible for the database" | "Designed and shipped a Postgres schema serving 12k daily queries" |
| "Helped with the frontend" | "Built the React dashboard that cut support tickets 30%" |
| "Worked on algorithms" | "Implemented 60+ data-structure solutions with documented complexity analysis (public portfolio linked)" |
| "Familiar with Python" | (skills line, not a bullet — bullets are achievements, not adjectives) |

Three rules:

1. **Every bullet is action verb + what you built + measurable scope or impact.** If you cannot quantify, name the scope ("a 14-week, 60-problem portfolio"). Vague verbs ("helped," "assisted," "was responsible for") are banned.
2. **One page until you have 8+ years of experience.** A recruiter spends roughly six seconds on the first pass. Two pages halves the attention each line gets.
3. **The projects section links the portfolio.** The CrunchTime portfolio is a project. Link it, describe it in one bullet in the strong shape, and let the link do the rest.

## On LinkedIn

Recruiters search by keyword and location. The profile is a search-optimization problem with four levers.

| Lever | Weak | Strong |
|-------|------|--------|
| Headline | "Aspiring software engineer \| Open to work" | "Software Engineer \| Python, Data Structures & Algorithms \| Building [domain] tools" |
| About | (empty, or third person) | First person, 3–4 short paragraphs: what you do, what you are looking for, the portfolio link |
| Featured | (empty) | The portfolio repo, pinned, with a one-line caption |
| Skills | (random or empty) | Ordered to match target job descriptions; the top three are the ones recruiters filter on |

The keyword discipline: pull the exact phrasings from the job descriptions you are targeting ("data structures," "algorithms," "Python," "distributed systems," whatever recurs) and make sure those exact phrases appear in your headline, About, and skills. Recruiter search is literal.

## On the target-company list

The list is the spine of the job search. An untargeted blast is the most common reason a strong candidate gets no replies.

- **Tier into reach / match / safety.** Reach: companies where your profile is below the typical bar but the upside is high. Match: companies where your profile fits the typical bar. Safety: companies where you are above the typical bar and likely to get an offer. The default split is **20% reach / 60% match / 20% safety**.
- **Name a team or product where you can.** "Stripe" is a company; "Stripe — the payments-reliability team" is a target. The named-team version makes your outreach specific and your "why this one" credible.
- **One "why this one" sentence per company.** If you cannot write a true, specific sentence about why this company, it does not belong on the list. The sentence is also the raw material for the outreach message.
- **25–40 companies.** Fewer than 25 and the funnel math does not produce enough interviews; more than 40 and the list stops being targeted.

## On outreach

The cold message that gets a reply is short, specific, and makes no ask beyond a 15-minute chat.

- **Short.** Four to six sentences. A recruiter reads the first two and decides.
- **Specific.** Reference something real — a product, a blog post, a team. Generic praise ("I love your mission") is filtered as spam.
- **No ask yet.** The first message asks only for a 15-minute conversation, not a referral, not a job. The ask comes after the relationship exists.
- **Link the portfolio.** One link, in context, as evidence — not as a wall of attachments.

The template trio (Lecture 3 §4): the cold recruiter message, the warm referral message, and the follow-up (sent 5–7 business days after no reply, once, then stop).

## On the application sprint and funnel math

Rough, order-of-magnitude funnel for a prepared candidate with a portfolio:

| Stage | Rough conversion |
|-------|------------------|
| Application → recruiter reply | ~1 in 8–10 (much higher for warm referrals) |
| Recruiter reply → phone screen | ~1 in 2 |
| Phone screen → onsite | ~1 in 3–4 |
| Onsite → offer | ~1 in 3 |

The takeaway: to land ~1 offer you want roughly 40–50 applications in flight, weighted heavily toward warm referrals where you can get them. This is why the target list is 25–40 and the homework is a 50-application sprint plan — the numbers are not arbitrary, they are the funnel.

## Recruiter-prep cheatsheet — the capstone checklist

The shape you should be able to walk in your head before you send a single application.

| Artifact | "Done" looks like |
|----------|-------------------|
| Public portfolio | Public repo; README answers who/what/start-here above the fold; index by pattern; 60+ entries; 3–4 flagship pieces |
| Mock #4 | Recorded; peer or platform; behavioral open + close; self-feedback note checks prior behavior changes stuck |
| Resume | One page; every bullet in action-verb + scope + impact shape; portfolio linked in projects |
| LinkedIn | Headline keyword-rich; About first-person with portfolio link; Featured pinned; skills ordered to targets |
| Target list | 25–40 companies; tiered 20/60/20; named team where possible; one "why this one" sentence each |
| Outreach | Trio written (cold recruiter / warm referral / follow-up); short, specific, no-ask-yet; portfolio linked |
| Stories | 90-second self-intro; "why now"; 3–5 STAR behavioral stories, all with a specific Result |

If every row is "done," you are recruiter-ready. Send the first message.

## Glossary additions

- **Portfolio (interview-prep sense)** — a curated, public, navigable collection of your UMPIRE write-ups, indexed so a hiring manager can assess your problem-solving in four minutes. Distinct from "a folder of files," which is raw material.
- **Public-ready** — the state of a write-up after the audit: no private asides, working links, a runnable code block, and a one-line summary. The difference between a drill note and a portfolio entry.
- **Flagship piece** — the three or four write-ups you point a reader to first. Your best work, chosen to show range (e.g., one DP, one graph, one design-adjacent).
- **Funnel** — the application → reply → screen → onsite → offer pipeline, viewed as conversion rates. The funnel math sets the number of applications you need in flight.
- **Tiering** — sorting target companies into reach / match / safety by how your profile compares to the typical bar. The 20/60/20 split is the default.
- **Reach / match / safety** — a reach is above your current bar (high upside, low odds); a match fits your bar; a safety is below your bar (high odds). Every target list needs all three.
- **Cold outreach** — a first-contact message to a recruiter or engineer you do not know. Short, specific, no-ask-yet, portfolio linked.
- **Warm referral** — an introduction through someone who already knows you or the company. Converts an order of magnitude better than cold outreach; worth disproportionate effort to obtain.
- **STAR** — Situation, Task, Action, Result. The shape of a behavioral-interview answer. The Result is the most-graded part and must be specific.
- **The 6-second skim** — the rough time a recruiter spends on the first pass of a resume. The one-page constraint and the strong bullet shape are both responses to it.
- **Exit mock** — Mock #4, the course's final mock; the closest simulation of a real onsite loop, with a behavioral open and close, run after three prior mocks have closed earlier behavior gaps.
